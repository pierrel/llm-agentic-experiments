# Delegate versus do\n\n- **Origin:** Pierre, 2026-08-25.\n- **Intervention:** Compare a fixed main-agent loop that completes a decomposable task itself with the same loop that breaks independent bounded chunks into subagent tasks, then checks and combines their results. Hold model, harness, tools, user request, fixture, decoding, total token and turn budgets, and final oracle fixed.\n- **Prediction:** Delegation may improve artifact success and completeness for tasks with several independent evidence-gathering or transformation chunks. It may add overhead, fail to coordinate, or harm tightly coupled tasks where the main agent needs the full shared state.\n- **Boundary:** A result must not be credited to extra tools, larger aggregate context, weaker oracle checks, or a leading user prompt. The study estimates one pinned model and harness, not an abstract claim that delegation always helps.\n- **Possible experiment:** Register matched hermetic tasks at increasing counts of independent chunks, with a natural outcome request and a deterministic final-artifact oracle. Capture each subagent input/output, main-agent aggregation, total turns, token use, and reason-coded failures. Keep a single-chunk negative-control stratum, then introduce task coupling as a separately powered axis.\n
